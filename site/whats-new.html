v0.33 6 October 2026 engine

Firefox and Safari find their way in

Firefox and Safari cannot share a tab's audio — their share dialog offers windows and screens only, and the sound is left out. The page now notices and shows those browsers the two ways in that work: listen with the microphone, picking the device that carries what the computer is playing (“Monitor of …” on Linux, “Stereo Mix” on Windows, a loopback device on a Mac), or play a file from this device. The message after a share with no sound says the same instead of a shrug. Chrome and the other Chromium browsers are unchanged.

v0.32 4 October 2026 engine

A slower director, and sharper recordings

The director now moves between three scenes — NAV, DUST and TORUS — and lets each one play for at least thirty seconds to a minute and a half before it looks for a change, so a scene has time to settle into the music instead of flipping every few bars. Drops still cut back to NAV at once and a build still parks there, waiting. The other scenes are still a key press away. A forced scene no longer sticks across a reload: open the page and the director is in charge again. Recordings made with R keep the full picture quality for the whole take and come down as one file with their notes inside.

v0.31 3 October 2026 engine

A what's-new page, and a line about what this is

Retina Rave now has a what's-new page: one short entry per version, newest first, going back to the first public build, written for someone who watches rather than reads code. v0.30 3 October 2026 engine

Sure of the key, a kaleidoscope on the beat, and a record button

The colour now follows the song's key only when the ears are sure of it: the bass has to agree with the harmony before the palette commits, so a wrong guess no longer flips the hue mid-track. MANDALA has been rebuilt from the ground up: the kaleidoscope turns one step per beat, kick, snare and hat each have their own voice in it, and the stretch before a drop tightens the fold and drains the colour until the drop lets go. Press R to record what you see and hear to a video file on your own device, and R again to stop; nothing is uploaded and there is no share button. DUST stays in step on tracks where the vocals carry the beat, and the code is now open under MIT with one clause of my own: if it runs at a party, I get in free, with a guest.

v0.23 1 October 2026 engine

True loudness, the right key, and a smoother beat

The beat's nudge is now a smooth velocity profile, so the picture bounces instead of jerking, and the clock no longer wanders off a live tempo. The key the colours follow is the song's tonic: until now the engine had been reading the fifth above it, so every key-coloured scene was a fifth off. Loudness is measured the way broadcast meters do it, so a drop that comes back louder after a quiet breakdown actually reads louder on screen. The kick that hides under a bass note is found, the hi-hat fires cleanly on sparkly passages, and the quiet void before a drop is drawn as one.

v0.20 30 September 2026 engine

The beat clock moves onto the raw samples

Tempo and beat phase are now worked out from the audio samples themselves, on the audio's own clock, rather than once per video frame, and filtered so one odd hit cannot yank the beat line. This is the new default: the beat holds steadier through a build-up and lands on the drop.
