RETINA RAVE

I taught a computer to listen to music.
This is what it sees.

Hi, I'm Toma. I like music and math and built this for fun.

Retina Rave is a music visualizer that runs in your browser. It listens to whatever you are playing and steers a camera through real mathematical objects: the shapes on screen are not drawn to look mathematical, they are the mathematics, computed live. Beats, drops and sections are detected as they happen, and a director picks the scene, remembers what each part of the track looked like, and crossfades between them. Nothing is random and nothing runs on a clock: every motion on screen traces back to the music.

The scenes

  • NAV — a walk over the Mandelbrot set's multiplier chart; each point the camera visits is a Julia set, and the music decides where it goes: the interval picks the bulb, the phrase walks its Farey neighbours, the kick jumps toward a Misiurewicz point, the sub presses the rim, the drop throws it out along an external ray, and the key colours it.
  • FEIGEN — a dive down the Feigenbaum period-doubling cascade, the road of ever-smaller copies along the real axis of the Mandelbrot set.
  • DUST — a swarm of particles in a flow field, with the fibres of the Hopf fibration threaded through it.
  • MANDALA — a box-fold fractal seen through a kaleidoscope.
  • TORUS — the Hopf fibration itself: circles on the three-dimensional sphere, projected down to where we can see them.
  • POLYTOPE — the regular four-dimensional polytopes, turning on the 3-sphere and dancing: the bass shoves the cage round, the rhythm runs in beads along its edges, and the twelve keys are its colours.
  • MAXWELL — Maxwell's four equations solved live on a grid: twelve charges by pitch class, a magnet turning one notch a beat, and every drum hit a real ripple of light.
  • NAV (v0.33) — key 9 is the v0.33 navigator, kept as the control: the same walk before the retune, so the two can be compared at one bar (key 1 against key 9).
  • GIELIS — the superformula: twelve nested shapes, one per note, that close up on every beat and open again in the silence.
  • CHLADNI — sand on a vibrating plate: the bass note draws the figure, every kick throws the sand, every slide melts one figure into the next.

How to use it

On a computer: play music in another browser tab, open retinarave.com, press Share a tab and pick that tab with its audio. Press ? at any time and the machine explains what you are looking at and what is moving it. On a phone: put the music on a speaker and let the page listen with the microphone.

Firefox and Safari cannot share a tab's audio — their share dialog offers windows and screens only, and the sound is left out (Mozilla bug 1541425, open since 2019). The page notices and offers the two ways in that work there: press Listen with the microphone and, in the browser's prompt, pick the device that carries what the computer is playing — Monitor of … on Linux, Stereo Mix on Windows where the sound driver offers it, a loopback driver such as BlackHole on a Mac — or play a file from this device. Chrome, Edge, Brave and the other Chromium browsers share tab audio directly.

How it works and what it does not do

Everything runs on this device. The audio is analysed inside your browser tab, frame by frame, and the picture is drawn by your own graphics card; nothing you play is sent anywhere, because there is no server on the other end to send it to — this site is a static page. When you press Share a tab, the dialog that opens is Chrome's own, and the page only ever receives the one tab you tick, with its audio, for as long as you leave it shared. The microphone works the same way: the sound stays in the page. A file you drop on the card is decoded in the page and never uploaded.

There are no accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no trackers and no ads. Nothing from a third party loads on these pages until you press play on a clip on what's new, and the recording below is the same: made on your device, never uploaded. Close the tab and there is nothing left behind.

Why? I built this for myself — I like music and math — and wanted to share it. Free and private is the point, not a phase.

Recording

Press R while it runs and R again to stop: what you saw and heard is saved as a video file on your own device, through your browser's normal download, with a small @retinarave mark in the corner. The recording is made on your computer and stays there; nothing is uploaded, there is no upload or share button, and this site never receives it. The music in your clip is the music you were playing, and the rights to it are yours to clear before you post it anywhere.
